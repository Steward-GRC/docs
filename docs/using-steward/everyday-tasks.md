# Everyday tasks

For everyday staff who use Steward to read, write and act on policies and procedures. You do not
need a special role to read documents; if you write or approve drafts, this page covers that too.
You reach Steward at your organisation's address and sign in with your username and password.

## Signing in and passwords

Open Steward at your organisation's address, enter your username and password, and select
**Sign in**. Once you are in, you land on your home dashboard. If sign-in fails, it almost always
means the username or password is wrong — try again carefully, watching for Caps Lock and stray
spaces. If it still fails, tell your administrator.

## Your home dashboard

When you sign in, you land on your **home dashboard** — your starting point, linking you straight
into the areas you are allowed to use: your policy and procedure library, search, drafts you are
authoring, approvals waiting on you, and compliance cases if you handle them. The dashboard is
personal: what it shows depends on your role and your access, so two people rarely see exactly
the same dashboard.

## Search

- **Search** — Type a word or phrase into the search box. Steward shows you policies and
  categories that match, grouped by type — the fastest way when you know part of the name or a
  keyword.
- **Browse the library** — Open the Policies or Procedures list to see every document you can
  read, or open a category to narrow down to its documents and its sub-categories.

## Acknowledge a document

When your organisation needs proof that you have read a document, it asks you to **acknowledge**
it:

1. **Open the document.** Find it through your dashboard, or through search.
2. **Read it**, from start to finish, not just the title.
3. **Confirm.** Select **Acknowledge** and confirm in the dialog. Steward records your name and
   the date.

## Author a draft

A draft is the first version of a document — a work in progress that only you and a few others
can see. Authoring is controlled per category, not by a single global role: you can create and
edit drafts in a category if you are an **owner** of that category (or a parent above it), or the
category's access rules grant you the **Author** action, directly or through a directory group
you belong to.

## Review and approve

For **approvers** — the people a workflow sends a draft to for a decision. Who can approve depends
on the document's category: approvers are either owners of that category, or users (or groups)
the category's access rules grant Approve access to. When an author submits a draft, it enters an
approval workflow and moves to the stage you are on; your **Approvals** page lists what is
pending and what is coming up.
